Chapter Fifteen

The Sermon of His Life

Pastor Charles stood behind the pulpit with his Bible open before him. For a moment, he said nothing.

The congregation waited. I could hear the small sounds that usually disappeared beneath conversation.

He looked older than he had the last time I saw him preach. There was something in his face that made me think of Moses descending the mountain, carrying what he had received.

His hand rested on the open page.

Kathy sat beside me, her fingers wrapped around mine. I could feel a slight tremble in her hand, or perhaps it was mine. The team was scattered nearby. Their presence reassured me, but the investigation that had brought us together now seemed far removed from this room.

We had come to church, where a pastor stood before a congregation with an open Bible. Whatever else we had learned, we were souls who needed the Gospel.

Pastor Charles lifted his eyes.

“Christ crucified,” he said. “Christ risen. That is the message before us today.”

The words settled over the congregation.

He looked down at the first page of Scripture.

“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.”

He paused.

“This is where the Gospel—the Good News of Jesus Christ—begins,” he said. “With the God who created us. Before man speaks, God is. Before creation moves, God commands. Before history begins, God reigns.”

“The Bible does not open with an argument for God. It opens with God. Before the mountains, the seas, or the stars, before time measured anything, God was. Creation came into being at His command.”

He turned a page slowly.

“And God said, ‘Let there be light,’ and there was light.”

Pastor Charles looked up.

“When God speaks, creation answers.”

He spoke without raising his voice, yet I found myself listening more closely.

“Light did not negotiate. Matter did not vote. Gravity did not organize a committee. When God spoke, existence obeyed. Scripture reveals the Creator, sovereign over all that is seen and unseen. He cannot be confined to the imagination of the creatures He made.”

He moved one hand across the open Bible.

“And if God is Creator, then nothing in creation is meaningless—not the world we have examined, not the lives we inhabit, and not you.”

Kathy’s hand tightened around mine.

“Psalm 19 tells us that the heavens declare the glory of God. Every sunrise bears testimony. Every star is a witness. The laws of nature answer to a Lawgiver who was never elected, never created, never corrected, and never dethroned.”

He turned to Job.

“God asked Job, ‘Where were you when I laid the foundation of the earth?’”

After reading the verse, he closed his eyes for a moment.

“That question was an act of mercy. Job was suffering and confused. He wanted answers. God revealed Himself.

“Sometimes what we need most in our suffering is a greater vision of God, even while our questions remain.”

The sanctuary remained still.

“Where were we when He laid the foundations of the earth? We were not there. But He was. Where were we when the morning stars sang together, when the oceans learned their boundaries, when the first atom answered its Maker?”

His voice softened.

“He was there.”

He waited before continuing.

“And the God who was there at the beginning is good. Genesis tells us He saw what He made, and it was good. Creation was not born evil. Matter was not a mistake. Life was not an accident. What God made bore the mark of His goodness.”

His tone changed.

“But we all know something happened. We do not live in Eden now.”

He looked across the room.

“Every honest person knows something is wrong with the world, and something is wrong in us. We can study the stars and still lie to our neighbor. We can map the genome and still envy, build cities and still hate, split atoms and still break our vows. An educated mind can serve a rebellious heart.

“Our intelligence has not delivered us from sin.”

The word landed with plain force.

“Sin is more than a mistake. Human brokenness and trauma are real, but they do not account for every choice we make. Sin is rebellion against a holy God. It is the creature telling the Creator, ‘I will decide what is good. I will decide what is evil. I will be my own authority.’”

He opened to Genesis again.

“In the garden, the serpent asked the ancient question: ‘Did God really say?’ Every generation hears that whisper. Is His Word true? Is obedience necessary? Is judgment coming? Is Christ the only way?”

Pastor Charles lifted his eyes.

“And every generation must answer.”

He let the question rest before continuing.

“But the same chapter that shows humanity falling into sin also gives the first promise of redemption.”

He read from Genesis 3:15, the promise that the seed of the woman would bruise the serpent’s head.

“Before Adam and Eve left the garden, God promised a Savior. Before the first grave was dug, He promised victory. Mercy was already being offered in the wreckage.

“That is the heart of the Bible: God coming to rescue people who could never reach Him by their own strength.”

He turned the pages slowly.

“Abraham heard that through his seed all nations would be blessed. In Moses’ day, the lamb’s blood marked the doorposts while judgment passed over. David received the promise of a Son whose throne would endure forever. Isaiah spoke of the child who would be born, the Son who would be given.”

Pastor Charles read from Isaiah 9, giving each name its full weight.

“Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.”

He looked up.

“Mighty God,” he repeated. “The promised Son would be more than a teacher, a prophet, or a moral example. Isaiah called Him Mighty God.”

Then he turned to Isaiah 7.

“Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.”

“God with us,” he said.

For a moment, he seemed unwilling to move beyond those words.

“God entering the world He made. Coming among the people who had turned from Him. Stepping into the ruins with saving purpose.”

He turned to Micah.

“But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah…”

He read the prophecy of the ruler whose coming forth was from old, from everlasting.

“God had named Bethlehem long before Caesar counted his empire. The place of Christ’s birth belonged to a purpose already declared.”

Then Daniel.

“One like a Son of Man came with the clouds of heaven, and to Him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom.”

Pastor Charles rested both hands on the pulpit.

“Do you see the road taking shape? The promises of the Old Testament lead toward Christ. Its sacrifices teach us to look for the Lamb. Its priests and kings prepare us to recognize His work and authority. The temple, the mercy seat, and the Passover all belong to the unfolding story of redemption.”

He turned to the Gospel of John.

“And then John writes, ‘In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.’”

His voice lowered.

“The One through whom creation came into being entered His creation.”

He read John 1:14.

“And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.”

Pastor Charles closed his eyes briefly.

“The Word became flesh. The Infinite became infant. The One who holds galaxies entered the womb of a virgin. The Maker of lungs took His first breath. The One who formed Mary was held in Mary’s arms.”

The congregation listened without stirring.

“Consider the glory of that.

“Jesus came among us as a Person. He walked our roads and knew hunger and weariness. He grieved and wept at a tomb. He touched lepers, welcomed children, and confronted demons. He forgave sinners and preached the kingdom, calling people to repent and believe the Gospel.”

Pastor Charles looked down at the page.

“John the Baptist saw Him and said, ‘Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world.’”

He paused.

“The Lamb of God. Those words tell us why He came. The lamb offered in sacrifice was given to die.

“No one ever taught as Jesus taught, healed as He healed, or loved as He loved. Yet His mission required more than teaching and healing. He came to give His life as a ransom for many.”

Pastor Charles opened to Isaiah 53.

“He was wounded for our transgressions. He was bruised for our iniquities. The chastisement for our peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed.”

He did not rush.

“Our transgressions. Our iniquities. Our peace. His stripes.”

He looked directly at the congregation.

“That is substitution. The innocent suffering for the guilty. The Holy One bearing the sins of the unholy. God demonstrated the terrible weight of sin when only the blood of His Son could answer it.”

I felt the words reach places in me I had defended for years.

“Jesus was betrayed by a friend and arrested by sinners,” Pastor Charles continued. “Cowards tried Him. Soldiers mocked Him. His own people rejected Him. Rome scourged Him and nailed Him to a cross outside Jerusalem.”

His voice trembled.

“The cross was the mission.”

He read from Luke.

“There they crucified Him.”

He closed the Bible gently over one finger to hold the place.

“Those four words carry the weight of the world. There they crucified Him. The hands that formed Adam were pierced. The feet that walked on water were nailed. The mouth that spoke forgiveness grew dry. The brow worthy of every crown wore thorns.”

He paused.

“And He stayed.”

That sentence nearly undid me.

“He could have called angels. He could have ended the world. He could have stepped down and left us to the judgment we deserved.”

Pastor Charles’s voice fell to a whisper.

“But love held Him there.”

I could barely look at him.

“Jesus suffered for sinners. He bore our guilt and carried our shame. He took the wrath we deserved so that mercy could come to us without violating the holiness of God.”

He turned to John 19.

“And when Jesus had received the sour wine, He said, ‘It is finished,’ and He bowed His head and gave up His spirit.”

Pastor Charles lifted his eyes.

“Finished. Nothing remained for us to add to His sacrifice. He had accomplished what He came to do.”

He repeated the word softly.

“Finished.”

The room seemed to hold it.

“The debt was paid and the sacrifice offered. He had fulfilled the law, struck the serpent, and opened the way.”

Then Pastor Charles turned the page.

“But the Gospel does not end with a sealed tomb.”

Joy began to show in his face.

“On the third day, women came to the tomb and found the stone rolled away. The opened tomb allowed witnesses to look inside and see that He was gone.

“The angel said, ‘He is not here, for He has risen, as He said.’”

Pastor Charles’s voice grew stronger.

“As He said.”

He looked around the room.

“Jesus knew what lay ahead. Death did not surprise Him, and the grave could not defeat Him. Neither Rome nor Satan could hold Him. Sin had no claim upon Him. Jesus Christ rose bodily, victoriously, and historically—and He lives eternally.”

He placed one hand over the Bible.

“If Christ is not raised, our faith is vain. But He is raised. Because He lives, we can receive forgiveness and mercy. Salvation and eternal life are more than things we wish were true.”

He leaned forward.

“Jesus is alive. And because He is alive, He still saves.”

Somewhere in the sanctuary, someone began to cry quietly. Pastor Charles continued.

“This is the Gospel. God made the world good, and man sinned against Him. God promised a Redeemer, and Christ came according to the Scriptures. He died for our sins, was buried, and rose again the third day. He ascended and now reigns. And He will come again.”

He turned to Acts.

“There is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved.”

He looked up.

“No other name.”

He spoke with the care of a doctor whose patient needed to hear the truth.

“Neither philosophy nor scientific knowledge can save you. Your achievements cannot wash away sin. Church attendance, a respectable family, and a moral reputation cannot do it either. Even your deepest sincerity cannot accomplish what only the Savior can do.”

His eyes moved across the room.

“Only Jesus.”

I felt those two words in my chest.

Only Jesus.

Pastor Charles closed his Bible halfway and rested both hands on it.

“And now we come to the invitation. This is the point toward which the whole message has been moving.

“The Gospel calls for a response. Jesus said, ‘Follow Me.’ He commanded us to repent and believe. We cannot answer that call by admiring His teaching from a comfortable distance.”

His voice grew warm and urgent.

“For some of you, what you have heard today has become personal. You know the Lord is calling you.

“You may have heard sermons your whole life. You know the stories and songs. Perhaps you can quote John 3:16, yet you have never surrendered your life to the One it reveals.”

He opened the Bible again and read:

“For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

Then he closed it.

“Whoever.”

He gave us time to hear it.

“That includes the church child who learned to behave but never surrendered to Christ. It includes the skeptic who came today with folded arms and a tired heart, and the man who has succeeded at everything except peace.

“It includes the woman who has carried shame so long she thinks it has become her name. It includes the religious person who knows the vocabulary of faith but has lost the joy of obedience.

“And it includes the backslider who wonders whether there is still time.”

My throat tightened.

Pastor Charles continued.

“There is still time.”

The words hurt, and in the same moment brought relief.

“Tomorrow has not been promised to you. But mercy is being offered now.”

He opened to 2 Corinthians.

“Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.”

His voice was gentle, more urgent than I remembered it being when he had preached loudly in years past.

“You do not have to make yourself clean before coming to Christ. You do not have to master every doctrine, put your life in order, or resolve every doubt. Come with the sin and shame you cannot remove. Bring your questions, your resistance, and your weariness. Bring the hidden thing you hope no one ever finds.”

He leaned forward.

“He already knows. And still He calls.”

Pastor Charles stepped out from behind the pulpit.

“Christ is able to save the lost and forgive the guilty. He can make those who are dead in sin alive and bring those who are far from God near.”

Tears gathered in his eyes.

“And this invitation is also for those who belong to Christ but have wandered. Perhaps you have grown cold or proud. Perhaps you have become so distracted that you scarcely noticed what was happening. You have maintained the appearance of faith while withholding your heart.”

Kathy’s hand tightened around mine.

“Return.”

The word reached the place Pastor Charles had told me it would.

“Return to your first love. Open His Word again. Pray honestly, and obey what He has already made plain. Come back to the cross and to the Savior who has never stopped reaching for you.”

His voice lowered.

“The Father knows your past. The Son is not surprised by your sin, and the Spirit is able to overcome your resistance. You have not exhausted the grace of God. The blood of Christ is enough.”

He waited, then lifted one hand, palm open.

“So I am going to ask plainly. If you need to be saved, come to Christ. Repent of your sin. Believe that He died for you and rose again. Confess Him as Lord. Your response must be to Him, whatever part this church or this sermon has played in bringing you here.”

He took a breath.

“If you need to return, return. Do not dress delay up as wisdom. Do not call pride caution or numbness maturity. If the Holy Spirit is dealing with you, obey Him.”

He looked down briefly, then back at us.

“In a moment, I am going to pray. After I pray, these altars will be open. You may need to come forward, or you may need to kneel where you are. Perhaps you need to take the hand of the person beside you and say, ‘I need Jesus.’ Perhaps your response will begin with the first honest prayer you have prayed in years.”

His voice was almost tender enough to break.

“But do not leave this room pretending you have not heard.”

He bowed his head.

“Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior of sinners, risen King, we ask You now to do what no preacher can do. Open blind eyes and soften hard hearts. Awaken those who are dead in sin. Save the lost and call Your wanderers home.

“Restore those whose love has grown cold. Bring healing to the ashamed and strength to the weary. Break through our pride, and let no one hide from mercy.”

The sanctuary was still.

“Father, draw them to Your Son. Holy Spirit, convict of sin, righteousness, and judgment. Show them Christ. Bring them beyond religious performance and fear alone to the Savior Himself—crucified, risen, reigning, and ready to save.”

He lifted his head. His face was wet, but his voice remained steady.

“The invitation is open.”

He looked across the room.

“If Christ is calling, come.”

For a while, no one moved. No music began, and Pastor Charles made no effort to hurry us.

I knew the sermon had brought us where a Gospel sermon should. We had heard of Christ and His mercy. Now each of us had to answer.

Kathy still held my hand.

I stared at the floor. I knew what was happening inside me, and I needed a moment to face it without looking around to see who might be watching. Through Pastor Charles’s preaching, the Word of God had found the locked rooms in me.

Return.

I knew Christ. I believed Him and belonged to Him. Yet I had delayed obedience to Him.

I had defended truths I had not fully obeyed. I had studied evidence while keeping surrender at a careful distance. Parts of my life remained things I considered mine to manage, protect, and excuse.

Now the Lord was calling me back in mercy.

There is still time.

When I looked at Kathy, she was already crying. She understood.

“Go,” she whispered.

I shook my head once, barely.

“Come with me.”

Her face softened, and she stood before I did.

We stepped into the aisle together. Obedience had become simpler than resistance.

I do not remember every step to the front. I remember the carpet beneath my shoes and the sound of Kathy breathing beside me. Pastor Charles watched us with tears in his eyes. He looked like a shepherd seeing someone he loved come home.

At the altar, I lowered myself to my knees. Kathy knelt beside me.

For a moment I had no words. Then they came.

“Lord Jesus,” I whispered, “I have believed You. But I have not yielded to You as I should. I have carried pride where I should have carried obedience. I have studied Your works while withholding pieces of my heart from Your rule. Forgive me. Restore me. Take all of me. Whatever time is left, let it belong to You.”

Kathy’s hand found my shoulder, and I bowed lower.

I saw no vision and heard no voice. I knew the deep mercy of being received by the One who had never stopped calling.

Then I heard hesitant footsteps behind me.

I kept my head bowed. Whoever was coming needed room to respond without becoming the object of everyone’s attention.

But Kathy looked, and her breath caught.

I turned.

Dawn was walking down the aisle.

Her face was pale and her eyes wet. One hand rested against her chest. Watching her, I thought of all the questions she had asked and the care with which she had considered each answer. Now she seemed to have reached something she could no longer hold at a distance.

Pastor Charles met her a few feet from where Kathy and I knelt. He gave her room.

“Dawn?”

She tried to answer, but no words came.

The woman who had spent her life studying language stood silent before the One from whom meaning came.

At last she whispered, “I understand now.”

Pastor Charles waited.

Dawn looked toward the open Bible on the pulpit, then down at the floor.

“It was never only meaning,” she said. “It was Someone speaking.”

Pastor Charles nodded, tears gathering again.

“And what is He saying to you?”

Dawn closed her eyes.

“That I need Him.”

“Then call on Him.”

She knelt slowly, not far from us, and folded her hands as though she were learning prayer and remembering it at the same time.

“Jesus,” she said, her voice trembling, “I believe You are the Son of God. I believe You died for my sins. I believe You rose again. I do not want to stand outside the truth anymore. Forgive me. Save me. Be Lord of my life.”

Pastor Charles bowed his head. Kathy wept softly beside me.

I remained on my knees, watching the Gospel do what evidence could never do by itself.

Evidence had brought us to the edge.

Christ had called us across.

Pastor Charles lifted one hand in blessing.

“Lord,” he prayed, “receive this confession. Restore this son. Welcome this daughter. Let the name of Jesus be praised in both.”

The sanctuary remained quiet for another long moment.

Somewhere behind us, someone whispered, “Amen.”

Another voice answered.

“Amen.”

The church had witnessed grace, and its quiet agreement was enough.
