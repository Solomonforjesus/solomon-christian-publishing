Author’s Note

This book began with a question—not a conclusion.

Not a manufactured controversy.

Not an attempt to force science into theology.

And not a sermon disguised as scientific inquiry.

It began with a question about whether the world we inhabit truly bears the marks of accident—or intention.

Over time, that question led through multiple disciplines: mathematics, cosmology, biological information, language, archaeology, morality, consciousness, quantum theory, and the difficult realities of human suffering itself.

What emerged was not a single argument, but a cumulative investigation.

In these pages, I use a term I eventually came to call Veritology: the disciplined investigation of reality, following truth wherever it leads.

That pursuit matters because modern conversations about science and faith are often framed incorrectly from the beginning. Science is frequently presented as the natural enemy of belief in God, while faith is portrayed as emotionally comforting but intellectually fragile. My experience led me elsewhere.

The deeper the investigation became, the more coherent reality appeared when understood through intelligence rather than accident, meaning rather than indifference, and ultimately God rather than randomness.

This book does not ask the reader to ignore science.

It asks the reader to follow the evidence honestly and completely.

Some of the people you will encounter in these pages are drawn from fragments of real conversations, real struggles, real skeptics, real believers, real grief, and real human searching. Certain names and details have been changed, but the emotional truths beneath them remain intact.

Many of us have quietly wrestled with the same questions:

Why does reality appear ordered?

Why does mathematics describe the universe so precisely?

Why does information exist in biological systems?

Why does morality feel objectively binding?

Why does consciousness resist reduction?

Why does suffering wound us so deeply if existence is ultimately indifferent?

And perhaps most importantly:

Why do so many roads of honest investigation continue pointing back toward Christ?

This work is unapologetically Christian.

Not because it begins with the assumption that Christianity must be protected, but because alternative explanations appeared increasingly insufficient to account for reality as we actually encounter it.

The chapters ahead do not offer simplistic answers to profound questions. Instead, they assemble observations, discoveries, disciplines, and human experiences the way an investigator assembles testimony.

No single chapter carries the entire case.

Together, however, a pattern begins to emerge.

Whether you approach these pages as a believer, skeptic, scientist, seeker, wounded soul, or curious observer, my hope is simple:

Follow the evidence honestly.

Follow the questions courageously.

And follow truth wherever it leads.

Let’s begin.
