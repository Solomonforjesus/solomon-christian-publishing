Chapter Sixteen

When Evidence Becomes Invitation

The pattern had formed slowly across the investigation. Different witnesses, working in different fields, had brought us toward the same conclusion.

Reality was not behaving like an accident.

By now, I could say that plainly. What proved harder to face was how far the evidence required me to follow.

A Designer might explain order, and a Creator might explain existence. A Lawgiver could account for moral obligation, while an Author could account for meaning. Yet recognizing those things did not heal the human condition.

The investigation had brought me to questions I could no longer keep at a distance. Where had the universe come from? What was wrong with us?

What was wrong with me?

That last question was the one I had worked hardest to avoid.

We have more information than we know how to use. We carry libraries in our pockets, yet we cannot cleanse our own hearts. We measure galaxies and map genomes while remaining capable of lies and hatred. We speak publicly of compassion while privately nursing envy, resentment, or desires we would be ashamed to name.

Ignorance alone cannot account for that.

The problem is sin.

The word sounds severe to modern ears, perhaps even primitive. Yet no softer word has explained our condition more accurately. A poor environment, illness, inherited trauma, and social pressure can wound us deeply. They may help explain what we carry and how we respond. They do not remove every responsibility for the choices we make.

Beneath our explanations remains the stubborn fact of moral guilt. We recognize it when others wrong us and feel it when conscience does its painful work. Our efforts to excuse ourselves reveal how much we know. We compare our conduct with someone worse, defend what we would condemn in another person, and hide what we cannot justify.

That was why evidence alone could never be enough.

Evidence can show that reality bears the marks of intention. It can point toward moral truth beyond preference and demonstrate that Jesus Christ cannot be dismissed as a figure detached from history.

But it cannot forgive sin or wash guilt from a soul. It cannot raise the dead or give a proud heart the humility it needs.

Evidence can bring a man to the threshold.

Only Christ can open the door.

For much of this journey, I had asked whether reality made better sense under a Creator than without one. After all the work, my answer was yes. The findings held together under God in a way they did not hold together without Him.

Christianity, however, makes a claim that reaches beyond the existence of God.

The God who made the universe has entered it.

If He were only the explanation behind cosmic order, we might admire Him from a distance. We could discuss the intelligence behind information in classrooms and laboratories, or consider the moral Lawgiver while trying to negotiate the terms of our obedience.

The Gospel brings us face to face with Him.

The Author stepped into His own story. The Creator took on flesh. The Judge came to bear the judgment sinners deserved.

Jesus Christ entered history as a man. He ate with sinners, touched lepers, and wept at a tomb. He exposed hypocrisy and forgave the guilty. He claimed authority no mere teacher had the right to claim, accepted worship, and spoke of judgment. He called God His Father and said that no one comes to the Father except through Him.

Then He went to the cross.

The cross confronts every shallow account of God. Love separated from holiness cannot explain why it was necessary. Holiness without love cannot explain why He would willingly endure it.

At Calvary, justice and mercy met without either being diminished. Sin was judged, and mercy was offered to the guilty. The debt was paid by the only One who owed nothing.

The Son of God stood in the place of sinners and bore what they deserved.

And then the tomb was empty.

The resurrection stands at the center of the Christian claim. If Christ is risen, death is not sovereign and sin does not have the final word. Despair cannot define a universe in which the Savior lives.

Every human life stands before Him.

That is where the question changes.

God has spoken through creation and conscience, through His acts in history, and most clearly through His Son. I could no longer treat the matter as something safely academic.

Since God has spoken, what will I do with what He has said?

I had to face that question. I ask you, as the reader, to face it too.

I cannot force an answer, and I have no desire to corner you into a confession. Faith cannot be manufactured by pressure. Repentance cannot be produced by a clever turn of phrase. A conviction borrowed from someone else will not sustain you through suffering, death, judgment, or eternity.

But I can ask you to consider the full weight of the question.

If the beginning was not an accident, then neither are you. If the universe bears the marks of intention, your life belongs within that purpose.

And if Jesus Christ is who He claimed to be, then the evidence we have examined must lead us beyond the stars, the genome, the constants, and the equations.

We must face the cross and the empty tomb.

For some people, that may be the hardest turn in the journey. We can debate design and causation, morality and consciousness, while keeping the discussion outside ourselves. The living Christ calls for something more personal.

He commands us to repent and believe.

The word repent has been abused by angry voices, but it is a word of mercy. We do not have to continue walking away from God. The road home is open. We can confess our guilt, receive forgiveness, and surrender the authority we have claimed over our own lives.

Repentance begins with admitting the truth about ourselves and turning to God. Faith allows us to trust Him even while questions remain, because Christ has answered our deepest need.

The Gospel offers more than religious improvement or another conclusion to add to a worldview. It calls us beyond using Jesus for comfort while retaining the right to rule ourselves.

Sinners can be reconciled to God because Jesus Christ died for sin and rose again.

That is the invitation: come to Christ.

Recognizing a door is not the same as walking through it.

This is where the book must end. The investigation has taken us through arguments and calculations, but none of them can take the Savior’s place. Establishing design does not heal the wound of sin. Showing why I believe atheism fails to explain reality does not, by itself, proclaim the Gospel.

The evidence became an invitation.

The invitation was Christ.

So I ask plainly:

What will you do with this?

You may dismiss it, though I do not think dismissal will be as easy as it once was. You may postpone it, though postponement also has consequences. You may study further, and there is no shame in honest study.

Perhaps you need to wrestle with what you have read. Perhaps your next step will be an uncertain prayer, an admission of doubt, or opening a Bible for the first time in years. You may need to sit quietly with truths you had hoped would make no claim upon you.

Take the question seriously. Give Christ the attention His claims demand. Leaving the matter unresolved does not place you beyond the need to answer Him.

If He is not risen, Christianity collapses.

But if He is risen, His authority reaches every part of life. Our theories and ambitions, our objections and wounds, the secrets we guard and the sins we excuse—all must be brought before Him.

My own heart included.

I write from no position above you. I followed the evidence because I needed the truth too. When that truth became personal, the hardest part was admitting how much I needed the God whose existence I had defended.

I do not know where this book finds you. I do not know what you have suffered or lost, what you regret, or what you have been afraid to ask.

You may have read as a believer, a skeptic, or someone who has wandered from a faith you once held closely. You may carry wounds from church. Perhaps you approached these pages as a scientist or student, intent on examining the arguments. Perhaps you simply hoped there might be more to reality than dust and death.

Whatever brought you here, I want to leave you with this:

You are not an accident, and no account of your chemistry exhausts who you are. You are not beyond the reach of grace.

The invitation is to the One through whom all truth holds together.

The beginning, the cross, and the empty tomb belong to His purpose. Perhaps your encounter with these words belongs within it too.

The evidence has done what evidence can do.

The invitation now stands.

Come to Christ.
