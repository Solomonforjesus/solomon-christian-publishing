Appendix A

How the Evidence Algorithm Worked

Before we ever ran the calculation, the team agreed on something important:
If we were going to build a tool to compare Directed Reality with Undirected Reality, it had to be fair, transparent, and open enough that even a critic could examine how it worked.

This was not about forcing a predetermined answer.
It was not about preaching through mathematics, disguising persuasion as computation, or quietly arranging the method to produce the outcome we preferred.

It was about a single disciplined question:

Under which explanatory framework is the accumulated evidence more expected?

Hᴰ—Directed Reality: a reality in which purposeful Mind stands behind the universe and in which order, intelligibility, life, information, consciousness, morality, and meaning are expected.

Hᵁ—Undirected Reality: a reality in which the universe and its contents arise through impersonal physical processes, with no purposeful mind built into reality’s foundation.

To keep the comparison level, we assigned the two hypotheses equal prior odds:
1:1
Neither Hᴰ nor Hᵁ began with greater overall probability than the other. That did not mean the models used identical internal parameter distributions. It meant only that the scale began level between the two competing hypotheses.

We called them:

Hᴰ—Directed Reality

Hᵁ—Undirected Reality

Then we asked the same question across multiple scientific fields.

1. What the algorithm actually did

Instead of focusing upon one discipline, we drew evidence from six distinct domains, each contributing part of the cumulative case. Together, they represented the major lines of inquiry developed throughout the book.

Each one compared:

If the universe is undirected, what should we expect to see?

If the universe is directed, what should we expect to see?

We assigned explicit likelihood judgments describing how expected each item of evidence would be under Hᴰ and under Hᵁ. The algorithm then combined those declared values according to the locked procedure.

No single domain carried the entire case.
Taken together, however, the evidence formed a cumulative pattern.

2. Guardrails (to keep the process honest)

To avoid fooling ourselves, we built in several safeguards:

Equal prior odds

Neither hypothesis began with greater overall probability than the other.

Pre-registration

We recorded the model definitions, assumptions, evidence categories, parameter choices, likelihood judgments, and decision rules before examining the final result.

Red-team challenge

A designated challenger argued the strongest reasonable case against the emerging conclusion and pressed the team to identify hidden assumptions, duplicated evidence, exaggerated weights, and weak definitions.

Visible judgments

The assigned likelihoods were stated openly rather than concealed inside the calculation, allowing readers and critics to challenge them directly.

Sensitivity testing

We altered reasonable assumptions and weights to determine whether the conclusion depended upon one fragile choice.

Noise and hardware records

The backend, shot count, routing information, measurement results, and known hardware limitations were preserved with the run.

3. What each field contributed

1. Cosmology and fundamental physics

The universe runs on exquisitely precise numbers: gravity strength, particle masses, electromagnetic charge, expansion rate, and more.
Change any of them slightly and life becomes impossible.

Under undirected reality, hitting these values is astronomically unlikely.

Under directed reality, a life-friendly universe is expected.

This domain provides large-scale structure—the “settings” of the cosmos.

2. Mathematics and intelligibility

Mathematics isn’t invented; it’s discovered.
Its truths are universal, unchanging, and accessible everywhere in the cosmos.

Math underlies:

physics

chemistry

probability

biological structure

information theory

language

logic

Under undirected reality, the existence of abstract, perfect, eternal truths is difficult to justify.

Under directed reality, mathematical order is exactly what you’d expect to find at the base of creation.

Math is the framework behind all the other fields.

3. Language and meaning

Across cultures, languages tend toward:

clarity

compressibility

error-reduction

one sound: one sign efficiency

These trends aren’t what random cultural drift typically produces.

Language behaves like a system that leans toward meaning, not noise.

4. Biology, information, and repair

Life runs on instructions—encoded information, error-correction machinery, molecular machines, repair systems, and regulatory logic.

Undirected processes can generate complexity.

But the information content and self-repairing architecture of life are far more expected under direction.

Matthew and Elise’s research gave our team real-world examples.

5. Archaeology and historical testimony

Archaeology tests whether Scripture’s historical claims are anchored in real places, people, offices, customs, and material settings.

Inscriptions, sites, manuscripts, and other remains provide points of comparison with the biblical record.

No artifact carries the whole case by itself, but taken together these historical convergences contribute to the cumulative pattern.

6. Consciousness, morality, and human dignity

Across every culture ever studied, humans converge on moral instincts:

justice

fairness

protection of the vulnerable

rejection of betrayal

condemnation of unjust harm

These act like something built into us, not invented by us.

Moral invariants point toward a deeper source.

4. Putting the fields together

When we combined all six domains—with safeguards, equal starting points, and extremely conservative assumptions—the balance of evidence shifted steadily.

A pattern emerged.

The scale didn’t tip because of emotion or preference.
It tipped because the world we actually live in fits one explanation better than the other.
