Chapter Fourteen

The Return

I. The Message

The house felt quiet after the run. I sat for a while without moving, aware that something had changed.

I set the AWL printouts on the table, straightened their edges once, and let them sit. They had done enough to bring me to the edge of something I could no longer pretend was merely academic.

I had spent much of my life believing in God, but belief is not always the same thing as surrender. A man can confess Christ with his mouth and still keep rooms of his heart locked through habit, fear, pride, or simple neglect. I knew the doctrines and the old hymns. I was familiar with Scripture and comfortable with the language of prayer.

But there are seasons when the Lord does not ask whether a man can explain Him.

He asks whether the man will follow.

My phone buzzed on the table.

Pastor Charles.

I stared at his name for a moment before opening the message.

Walter,

The Lord has made the matter plain to me.

This Sunday I will preach a message I have carried for years but have never been released to deliver.

Your calculation will be my introduction.

The message is Christ—crucified, resurrected, reigning.

From the Old Testament to the New, I will speak of God’s sovereignty and redemption, the Breath of God, the Word made flesh, and the Spirit calling men to life.

I thought you should know first.

And, Walter… prepare your heart.

Something is coming for you.

—Pastor Charles

I read it three times, slowing down on the last.

Prepare your heart.

The words unsettled me. Fear might have made me run, but this felt like conviction, holding me still when running would no longer be honest.

For the first time in a long while, I received Pastor Charles’s words as something more than religious encouragement. They were aimed at me. He was inviting me to respond, and somehow that was harder to face than an accusation.

II. Old Memories Surface

I leaned back in my chair, and memories began to surface.

They were quiet, almost ordinary moments, the sort I rarely thought to share.

I remembered a storm from childhood, when I had been afraid beyond words yet somehow felt watched over. Then a boy from middle school who spoke about Jesus as though he knew Him personally. And a night under a wide sky when I had felt the smallness of my own life and the nearness of Someone I refused to name.

I remembered Kathy praying in another room when she thought I could not hear her. She had whispered my name before the Lord with a tenderness that had outlived my stubbornness. I had never told her I heard.

For years, I had carried that moment privately—the sound of someone asking God for something in me I was not yet willing to ask for myself.

There had been times I should have been hurt worse than I was. A diagnosis that did not become what it might have become. Warnings and unexpected words that arrived when I needed them.

I had attributed those things to coincidence, luck, instinct, or good timing.

After everything we had seen, those explanations felt thin. Turning every moment into a sign would be foolish. Yet a life can become so crowded with mercy that dismissing it requires more effort than receiving it.

The evidence had stripped away some of my excuses. Pastor Charles’s message was exposing the indifference beneath them.

That hurt more than I expected.

I put my elbows on the table and covered my face with both hands.

“Lord,” I whispered, “what are You doing?”

No answer came then. I sat with the weight of the question.

III. The Call

The phone buzzed again. This time Pastor Charles was calling.

I answered before I could talk myself out of it.

“Walter,” he said, calm and steady, “I’m glad you saw the message.”

“I saw it.”

“How do you feel?”

My old instinct was to make a joke or find a safe answer. But I was too tired to pretend.

“Unsteady,” I said.

Pastor Charles let out a soft breath. “Good.”

“Good?”

“Yes,” he said. “Sometimes that means the Lord is near.”

I swallowed hard.

“You said something is coming for me.”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“A return.”

I had expected something harsher, perhaps a warning, or some assignment I was unprepared to accept. His answer left me unsure what to say.

“I’m not sure I understand.”

“You belong to Christ, Walter. But there are places in you that have lived like orphans. The Lord is not calling you into a new religion. He is calling you home to the One you already know.”

The room seemed to narrow around the sound of his voice.

“I don’t know how to do that,” I said.

“You don’t need to know everything,” Pastor Charles said. “You need to listen.”

“To what?”

There was a pause.

“To Him.”

When the call ended, I sat with the phone still against my ear.

Pastor Charles had known me long enough to understand when to stop talking. He left me with the question God was already pressing.

IV. There Is Still Time

I had encountered that invitation before, years earlier, in a vision.

After a hard day’s work, I had lain down to rest and drifted into sleep. What came to me was unlike an ordinary dream. Its clarity stayed with me long afterward. I remembered it because it had confronted me with my life and offered rescue in the same breath.

Scenes began moving through my mind, vivid and relentless. I saw my work and the success it had brought, the comfort and possessions I had acquired. My family and friends were there, along with the respect I had come to enjoy.

By every ordinary measure, I had lived a successful life. I had accumulated some things. I had known hardship, but nothing that had destroyed my household. I had not lost my wife or buried my children. I had food and shelter, a reputation, and the stability men work years to build.

Yet as the scenes continued, I grew restless. I began to see what my pursuit of that life had cost me.

Then I noticed him.

Many years before, he had promised me a good life, full and satisfying, mine to manage and enjoy. He had not appeared dangerous then. Deception rarely does at first.

Now the mask had come off. He looked aged and repulsive, twisted by malice and time.

Only then did I realize where I was standing: at the brink of a huge, smoldering pit.

The fire was unquenchable. Smoke filled the air, along with the terror of souls who had believed the lie until they could no longer deny where it had brought them. The finality of it overwhelmed me.

The one I had followed turned toward me.

“It is time,” he said.

His meaning was clear. I was to jump.

From the other side of the pit, I heard a still, small voice.

I looked across and saw One standing there, dressed in a brilliant white robe, His hand outstretched toward me.

“There is still time,” He said.

His quiet words carried authority no scream could imitate.

“You now see the lies of your father. Your destiny is still yours.”

The old deceiver charged at me, furious that I had heard the truth. He came straight for me as if to shove me over the brink into the only hell I would ever have known.

I fell, tumbling head over heels into the abyss.

And then I awoke.

My body was still. My heart was not. The words of Scripture moved through my mind as clearly as if they had been written across the room:

“For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16.

I had known that verse.

But in that moment, it knew me.

There had been One reaching for me my whole life, and I had ignored Him more often than I wanted to admit. Even as a man who believed and could speak the language of faith, I had treated His mercy too lightly. Comfort had dulled my obedience. I had mistaken pride for strength and success for safety.

But He had not stopped reaching.

There is still time.

After Pastor Charles’s call, with the evidence behind me and Sunday ahead, I finally understood why that vision had never left me. Its terror had served a merciful purpose. I had needed to wake up.

The Lord had rescued me from the father of lies and from my own arrogant heart. He had exposed the terrible illusion that a full life without full surrender was enough.

I bowed my head and whispered the only prayer I could honestly form.

“Thank You, Lord, for pursuing me. Thank You for loving me. Thank You for Your patience and grace. Do not let me waste the time You have given.”

I saw no sign and heard no rushing wind. A quiet peace settled inside me, deep and unmistakable. I still had questions, but I had stopped resisting the One who knew their answers.

V. Kathy

When I found Kathy in the kitchen, she knew before I said much of anything.

That has always been one of the holy irritations of marriage. A wife who has prayed for a man long enough can sometimes read what God is doing in him before he has language for it.

She stood near the counter, looking at me with the careful tenderness that had steadied our life more times than I could count.

“You look different,” she said.

I tried to smile. “Long few days.”

She shook her head.

“No. That’s not it.”

I looked away.

She stepped closer and rested one hand against my chest. Beneath it was a heart that had spent too many years guarding itself from the very God it claimed to trust.

“I’ve been praying for you,” she said.

“I know.”

“Not for a crisis,” she whispered. “For clarity.”

That nearly broke me.

I lowered my head.

“I think the Lord is calling me back to Himself,” I said. “Not because I didn’t believe. I did. I do. But I think I have kept too much of myself outside the reach of obedience.”

Kathy’s eyes filled.

“What did He say?”

I swallowed.

“That there is still time.”

She covered her mouth with one hand and closed her eyes. For a moment neither of us spoke.

Then she stepped into me, rested her forehead against mine, and said, “Then we walk forward.”

She could have reminded me how long she had waited or how often I had resisted. Instead, she stood with me. I felt the grace in that, and the strength of the covenant we shared. She had been praying while I had been analyzing.

For the first time in longer than I cared to admit, I felt us turning together toward the same purpose. We had always shared affection, history, and responsibilities. Now something long out of alignment had begun to come right.

There was work ahead, but the change was real.

VI. The Summons

That night, I sent the message.

Pastor Charles is delivering a special message this Sunday.

It ties together everything—AWL, Amplitude Weighted Logic; the science; the discoveries; and something deeper.

I am asking all of you to come. Your support will mean something to him, but I also believe this message is for us.

—Walter

The replies began to arrive. A few came immediately; others took longer.

Steve said he would be there.

Veronica answered with a single line: “I think I need to hear this.”

Dawn wrote back later than the others.

“I’ll come.”

I read that one twice.

Matthew said he and Elise would be there. Hal replied that he would not miss it, and Kenji Yokosuka said he would arrive early.

Scott Tyson sent only “Yes” at first. A minute later, another message came through.

“I think we all know this was never only about the calculation.”

He was right.

The science had mattered. So had the arguments and discoveries, the calculations we checked, and the questions that made us uncomfortable. None of that work had been wasted.

The evidence had given us a road to follow.

And the road was leading to Christ.

VII. The Days Before Sunday

The days before Sunday passed slowly. Ordinary moments carried a weight I had seldom noticed before.

Each morning I woke earlier than usual and sat with coffee before the house stirred. I left the AWL printouts and my notes alone. For once, I felt no need to chase another argument or strengthen the case.

The investigation had done its work. Now the Lord was dealing with me.

Kathy often joined me without asking questions. She would sit beside me, or rest a hand on my shoulder before going back inside. Sometimes we prayed together, though my prayers were still clumsy and uneven.

I was beginning to understand that I could bring Him an honest prayer without first making it sound accomplished.

At night, I found myself thinking about the word Pastor Charles had used.

A return.

I had already confessed Christ and believed the Gospel. This was not my first step out of unbelief. But I had neglected the faith I professed. Obedience had become something I understood more readily than I practiced. I knew the Shepherd’s voice, yet I had wandered to the edge of the field.

There is still time.

That sentence stayed with me, calling me out of the wandering without excusing it.

Saturday night, I lay awake beside Kathy, listening to her steady breathing and the faint sounds of the house settling.

She stirred when I sat up.

“You okay?” she asked.

“I think so.”

“That means no.”

I laughed quietly.

She reached for my hand in the dark.

“I’m ready,” I said after a while.

“For tomorrow?”

“For whatever God wants.”

Her thumb moved gently across the back of my hand.

“I’ll be with you.”

I nodded, though she could barely see it.

“I know.”

AWL and the long investigation had helped bring us to this point. Tomorrow, I would have to face what that journey meant for my own response to Jesus.

The thought both steadied and frightened me.

VIII. The Threshold

On Sunday morning, I woke with a clear sense of why we were going.

Still, I stood in front of the mirror longer than usual, adjusting a shirt collar that did not need adjusting. I recognized the man looking back at me, including things about him I had avoided seeing. I had believed, and I had wandered. Now the Lord was calling me back.

I whispered, “Lord, do not let me miss You.”

Kathy appeared in the doorway behind me.

“You ready?”

I turned.

“No,” I said.

She smiled.

“But I’m willing.”

“That will do,” she said.

We drove to church in a comfortable silence. I prayed without trying to put every thought into words.

Several of the others were already there when we pulled into the lot. Seeing them made the invitation I had sent feel suddenly real.

Steve stood near the entrance, solemn for once. Veronica walked in with a notebook tucked under one arm, though I doubted she would take many notes.

Matthew and Elise spoke softly near the doors. Hal waited with his Bible in hand, looking as though he expected to hear testimony that concerned him personally.

Kenji stood slightly apart, observant and respectful. He seemed to sense the weight of the morning even if he did not yet have words for it.

Scott Tyson gave me a nod that said enough.

Then I saw Dawn.

She stood near the walkway, hands folded, looking from the church doors to the people around her. She seemed thoughtful, perhaps guarded. But she had come, and that mattered.

I let her have the moment. There would be time to speak.

Inside, the sanctuary was already filling. Older men in worn jackets sat near young couples and mothers with children. Some people watched with folded arms. Others had heard a thousand sermons and still needed the Word like bread.

Soft music gave the room a reverent quiet.

Kathy and I sat together. The others settled nearby, scattered across the rows but close enough for me to feel the mercy of their presence.

Before the service began, Pastor Charles stepped out from the side hallway. He carried the same Bible and moved with his familiar calm, though his expression was unusually grave.

He saw me and walked over, resting his hand on my shoulder.

“Walter,” he said, “today is the day the Lord appointed for this message.”

I nodded, unable to answer.

His eyes moved across the team, then back to me.

“What God began exposing through evidence,” he said, “He will now answer through His Word.”

A shiver moved through me.

He lowered his voice.

“Are you ready?”

“No,” I whispered. “But I’m willing.”

Pastor Charles smiled.

“That is all He asks of you today.”

Then he turned and walked to the pulpit.

As the room quieted, Kathy took my hand. I looked toward the front and felt again the mercy that had met me in the quiet.

There is still time.

Pastor Charles opened his Bible. The pages rustled softly as he looked over the congregation, then down at the Word of God.

When he spoke, his voice was steady with conviction.
