Can Science Point to God? Questions About Design, Evidence, and Reality

Did the universe come about by chance, or does its order suggest intention? Can mathematics tell us anything about why nature is intelligible? Does the information in living cells raise questions beyond chemistry? These are serious questions with scientific, philosophical, and theological dimensions. They deserve careful investigation, not slogans.

Fingerprint of Reality: Science, Design, and the Truth Beyond Matter, a free book by Gary Dearing, follows these questions through cosmology, mathematics, biology, language, quantum computation, archaeology, morality, and consciousness. It considers how different lines of reasoning may contribute to a cumulative argument for intentional design and ultimately presents the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

Did the universe have a beginning?

Modern cosmology describes the evolution of the observable universe from an early hot, dense state. Questions about why there is a universe at all, why the laws of physics take the form they do, and whether a beginning requires an explanation extend into philosophy. Fingerprint of Reality uses these questions as an opening to examine whether reality is self-explanatory or points beyond itself.

Does fine-tuning suggest an intelligent designer?

Physical constants and conditions are often discussed in relation to the possibility of complex structures and life. The interpretation of fine-tuning is contested: scientists and philosophers consider selection effects, deeper physical laws, multiverse hypotheses, and purposeful design. A thoughtful inquiry must distinguish measurements from competing explanations. Explore this theme in the book and in our article on fine-tuning.

Does DNA contain information, and what does that mean?

DNA stores biological sequences that cells copy and use in complex molecular processes. Evolutionary biology investigates how genetic variation and selection shape living organisms; discussions of design ask whether biological information has broader philosophical significance. The book approaches the living cell as one part of a larger investigation, not as an isolated proof.

Why can mathematics describe the universe?

Mathematics predicts physical phenomena with remarkable effectiveness. That success prompts questions about abstract truths, the order of nature, and whether an intelligible universe has a deeper foundation. The answers are not settled by one theorem or one equation, but the questions are central to the book's exploration.

Can consciousness and morality be explained by matter alone?

Neuroscience studies the biological correlates of experience, while philosophy asks what subjective awareness is and why it exists. Ethics similarly examines how moral judgments arise and whether moral truths are objective. These remain active areas of inquiry. The book explores what they might imply about human identity, meaning, and responsibility.

What does quantum computing have to do with faith?

Quantum computers use physical phenomena to carry out computations. Their behavior does not establish supernatural intervention. In Fingerprint of Reality, a quantum optimization experiment becomes part of a broader narrative about evidence, inference, and how we reason under uncertainty. The distinction between experimental results and theological interpretation is important.

Where does the investigation lead?

Evidence and interpretation are not the same thing. Readers may weigh the arguments differently. Gary Dearing invites readers to follow the questions honestly, then consider the Christian claim that the source of meaning is not an impersonal force but the living God revealed in Jesus Christ.
